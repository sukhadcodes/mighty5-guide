Stop 2 · Hoodoos & amphitheater rim

Bryce Canyon National Park

The world's largest concentration of hoodoos, seen from a high rim trail or from the amphitheater floor looking straight up at them.

Best overall time to visit: April–May or September–October
  • 01

    Navajo Loop Trail

    The park's most popular single trail — drops from Sunset Point through Wall Street's narrow switchbacks past Thor's Hammer and Twin Bridges.

    Distance
    1.4–1.5 mi loop
    Elevation
    ~550 ft gain
    Difficulty
    Moderate
    Time needed
    1–2 hrs

    Best time: Morning; Wall Street sometimes closes in icy winter conditions.

  • 02

    Queen's Garden + Navajo Combo

    The classic combo loop below the rim, connecting Sunrise and Sunset Points past Queen Victoria and Gulliver's Castle formations.

    Distance
    ~3 mi loop
    Elevation
    ~600 ft gain
    Difficulty
    Moderate
    Time needed
    1.5–2.5 hrs

    Best time: Early morning to beat both heat and crowds, spring or fall.

  • 03

    Rim Trail (Sunrise–Sunset Pt.)

    A paved, mostly flat section of the longer Rim Trail linking the two main viewpoints along the amphitheater's edge — accessible to wheelchairs and strollers.

    Distance
    1 mi RT
    Elevation
    Minimal
    Difficulty
    Easy
    Time needed
    ~30 min

    Best time: Sunrise at Sunrise Point, sunset at Sunset/Inspiration Point.

  • 04

    Peekaboo Loop Trail

    A longer loop below the rim past the Wall of Windows, the Three Wise Men and the Cathedral formations — shared with horseback tours.

    Distance
    5.2–5.5 mi loop
    Elevation
    ~1,500 ft gain
    Difficulty
    Strenuous
    Time needed
    3–4 hrs

    Best time: Spring or fall mornings; carry more water than you think you need.

  • 05

    Fairyland Loop Trail

    A quieter loop through the Fairyland amphitheater with far fewer crowds than the main Navajo/Queen's Garden circuit.

    Distance
    8 mi loop
    Elevation
    ~1,700 ft gain
    Difficulty
    Strenuous
    Time needed
    4–5 hrs

    Best time: Cooler months or an early spring/fall start.

  • 06

    Bristlecone Loop Trail

    A short forest loop at the park's highest point among thousand-year-old bristlecone pines — cool even in summer.

    Distance
    1 mi loop
    Elevation
    Minimal
    Difficulty
    Easy
    Time needed
    ~30–45 min

    Best time: Midday in summer, when the high elevation forest offers relief from the heat.
